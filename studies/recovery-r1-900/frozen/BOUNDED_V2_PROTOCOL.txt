BOUNDED FOUR-ROTOR R1 SENSITIVITY — V2, 13 NOVEMBER 2026

Research question
With a physically realizable healthy-quad allocation and the retained controller,
how sensitive are modeled release-recovery outcomes to the historical plant and
the two frozen R1 catalog/CAD plants? This is a computational sensitivity study.
It does not qualify measured propulsion authority, a platform or a learned warning.

Allocation: exact realized forces, not independent axis clips
For motor i at(x_i,y_i), spin vector(+,-,+,-), define
  B = [1; y_i; -x_i; kappa*spin_i], w=(T,tau_x,tau_y,tau_z)=B f.
Assumed kappa=.004/(nominal_Tmax/2), following the historical placeholder yaw
bound convention and existing failure_allocation.py. This is unmeasured, and
spin assignment is a conventional assumption, not a confirmed wiring layout.
At demanded collective T, compute f0=B^-1(T,0,0,0), df=B^-1(0,tau_cmd).
Find the largest common lambda in[0,1] satisfying0<=f0+lambda*df<=Tmax_available/4.
Return the actual B f wrench. Collective is preserved; all3 requested moments
share one direction-preserving scaling. This is a conservative healthy allocation
policy, not a torque-optimal or rotor-failure allocator. Yaw demand can consume
roll/pitch headroom. No force is silently clipped into feasibility beyond1e-12
floating-point boundary cleanup. Every integration step checks bounds, finite
values, force closure and moment closure. External torque_bias remains a sampled
disturbance approximation, not measured rotor-to-rotor effectiveness mismatch.

Retained controller and dynamics
Keep original attitude PD gains plus gated integral, original altitude PD,
25% inverted control floor and75% collective ceiling. No gain/limit retuning.
Use full Euler I*omega_dot=tau-omega cross(I*omega) and quaternion kinematics.
Nominal50ms detection and60ms motor-start dead times remain unchanged. There
is NO continuous actuator lag. CG moment uses negative CAD CGxy plus uncertainty
offset: r cross(0,0,T). No descent inflow/ground effect/voltage dynamics is added.
